\begin{abstract}

Hard disk drives remain among the most frequently replaced components in storage systems, and their unexpected failure causes data loss, downtime and increased operational cost. Manufacturer-embedded SMART thresholds detect only 3--10\% of failures at acceptably low false-alarm rates. Classical methods (SVM or Naive Bayes) perform reasonably on SMART data, but most of them rely on a single model and do not combine different learning strategies. Our combination of all 4 independent models - random forest classifier, an unsupervised autoencoder anomaly detector, a UMAP clustering model and another classifier, made on the bottleneck from autoencoder. Each model captures different kind of failure signal, and each one has its own interpretational value. At the end, all 4 predictions are combined into single aggregated health index (AHI).
