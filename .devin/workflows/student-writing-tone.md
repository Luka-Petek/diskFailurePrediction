## Context
This project should read like a student paper from an early-stage researcher. Improve clarity and correctness, but avoid over-polishing or adding advanced jargon.

## Steps
1. Identify what actually needs fixing.
   - Read the target section and mark sentences with unclear wording, grammar issues, or placeholders.
   - Do not rewrite sentences that already read fine.

2. Edit minimally.
   - Prefer small, local edits over full rephrasing.
   - Keep the author's structure and flow when possible.
   - Avoid high-register synonyms (use "use" not "utilize"; "show" not "demonstrate").

3. Tone rules.
   - Student-friendly vocabulary; short-to-medium sentences.
   - Active voice where natural; don't force it.
   - Be modest and precise about claims; no hype.
   - If exact numbers are not central, keep them general (as agreed for counts).

4. Technical accuracy.
   - Do not invent results or citations; keep placeholders only until replaced with verified info.
   - Preserve citations and their placement.
   - If a simplification may reduce correctness (e.g., naming algorithms), ask before changing the author's choice. Otherwise, respect it.

5. Apply across the whole project.
   - Paper sections (Abstract, Introduction, Methodology, Results, Discussion, Conclusion), README, and code comments.

6. Final checklist before committing.
   - Would this still sound like a beginning student wrote it?
   - Are claims honest and not overstated?
   - Are key details correct, placeholders resolved, and counts generalized where appropriate?
   - Did I avoid over-stylizing the prose?

## Usage
- Skim this workflow before any editing pass.
- After edits, re-check using the checklist. Commit minimal diffs.
